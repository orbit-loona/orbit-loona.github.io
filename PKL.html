Proto-Kegedo-Leiykan

Proto-Kegedo-Leiykan was a language spoken in and around present day Eirenia at an estimated date between 6,200 and 4,200 B.C.(E.), being the ancestor of the Kegedo-Leiykans languages. It is known to have been in use by 5,200 B.C.(E.); I guess it's oddly convenient that those paper dictionaries the Proto-Kegedo-Leiykans left got fossilized in that bog in West Slovenia...

Phonotactics

The maximal syllable structure is (C)V(C). Codas are rare. Consecutive vowels are allowed. Plosives and /ɾ/ are not allowed as codas.

Grammar

Proto-Kegedo-Leiykan marks case (nominative, accusative, genitive (marking both possessorness and modifierness for nouns), instrumental, comitative, illative, and elative); case markings must agree in number with their nouns. Personal pronouns can be dropped when context or conjugation makes the referent clear, especially in imperative statements. Though it marks cases, Proto-Kegedo-Leiykan but defaults to an SVO order. It places adjectives and adverbs* after what they modify, and prepositional phrases are treated as adjectives or adverbs. When there is an indirect object, it comes before the direct object. It is partly fusional, and verb endings (which mark number and aspect) must agree with the performer in number; there are some suffixes which indicate mood, which are exempt and do not decline for number. The imperative mood implies the present tense. Indefiniteness is implicit, and there is a definiteness marker prefix.

* Exceedingly few words can be classified as adverbs; as there is no grammatical distinction between adjectives and adverbs, only the words on which it does not make sense to modify a noun could really be called adverbs.
